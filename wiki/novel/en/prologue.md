# PROLOGUE

**Terra is a world alive with Aethra.**

It flows with the rivers, rests deep beneath the earth, and rides the wind to the highest mountain peaks.

It dwells in growing trees, in monsters roaming the wilds, and in every creature born into this world.

No one truly knows where Aethra comes from.

Yet all life on Terra knows its influence.

From Aethra, magic is born.

With Aethra, a Healer can close a wound that should have taken a life. A Mage can turn fire into a weapon. A Spellblade can channel it through the edge of a sword.

And for some creatures, Aethra can do something greater.

It can change life itself.

Bodies adapt.

Legacies sleeping within the blood can awaken.

A creature born in one form may grow into something else.

That is how life evolves on Terra.

But life has never followed only one path.

Humans raised kingdoms across broad plains and great rivers.

Elves built civilizations among ancient forests.

Dwarves forged their cities deep beneath the mountains.

Dragonkind lived in lands few other peoples dared enter.

And beyond them lived so many other peoples that even Terra's greatest maps had never truly been complete.

They had languages.

Families.

Beliefs.

Traditions.

And places they called home.

Some traded with one another.

Some forged alliances.

Some lived without ever troubling their neighbors.

And some had fought for so long that no one remembered who first raised a weapon.

Kingdoms rose.

Alliances formed.

Borders shifted.

Peoples who had once been friends could become enemies.

And those regarded as enemies for generations might one day stand on the same side.

For Aethra never chose who deserved to receive it.

It flowed to those who built.

To those who destroyed.

To those who protected.

And to those who conquered.

Aethra gave life.

Aethra gave possibility.

But what life chose to do with that possibility—

**was always its own choice.**

### Forty-Three Years Ago

### Fort Asteron — Southern Frontier of the Kingdom of Eldoria

The fortress trumpet sounded three times from the southern tower.

The first note stopped every conversation.

The second sent soldiers reaching for their weapons.

By the third, Fort Asteron's southern gate was closing.

From atop the wall, a young soldier stared across the southern plain and, for a moment, forgot how to breathe.

The horizon was moving.

Not a storm.

Nor a horde of monsters pouring from the Monster Forest.

It was an army.

Thousands of figures emerged through the morning mist.

Massive Ogres marched with axes and clubs. Ranks of Orcs followed with long spears. Among them moved other bands of Demonkind from the southern territories.

Dark banners flew above their ranks.

But only one drained the color from the veterans' faces.

Deep crimson cloth.

At its center, a single symbol.

**β**

A captain on the wall whispered,

"Beta."

The young soldier beside him swallowed.

The rumors of the past several weeks were true.

An army from the south had broken through Eldoria's frontier.

And they had not come to raid a border village.

They had come as conquerors.

Drums sounded in the distance.

Once.

Then again.

Thousands of warriors began moving almost as one.

The captain gripped the stone parapet.

He had faced Orcs before.

Hunted Ogres.

He had even fought monsters far larger than himself.

But this was different.

These were not scattered tribes that happened to be moving in the same direction.

Someone had united them.

Someone had brought them all the way to Asteron.

And whoever that person was—

they had yet to show themselves.

The trumpet sounded again.

"EVERYONE TO YOUR POSITIONS!"

The Beta army broke into a run.

The battle for Asteron had begun.

## Day One

That morning, thousands struggled to leave Asteron.

The fort had long since become more than a military outpost.

Homes stood behind its walls. A market had grown between warehouses and barracks. Smithies served soldiers and farmers alike. Families had settled there. Children had been born knowing Asteron as nothing but home.

Now the road to the northern gate was packed with wagons.

Horses whinnied amid the crowd. A mother dragged two children through the street. Merchants abandoned whatever they could not carry.

Soldiers shouted for everyone to keep moving.

In the command room, Rolan stood before a large map.

Small stones marked Eldoria's positions.

There were not enough.

There were never enough.

"The northern gate?"

"Evacuation has begun," an officer replied. "Most civilians should be out before nightfall."

"Keep the road open."

"What if they attack before the evacuation is complete?"

Rolan stared at the southern line on the map.

"Then we make sure they never reach the gate."

A messenger rushed inside.

"Commander!"

"Reinforcements?"

"Solmaria is already moving, but they're still several days away."

"Verenza?"

"Mustering troops."

"Astrelia?"

"No confirmation yet."

"Auregrad?"

"Their cavalry has departed."

"How long?"

The messenger hesitated.

"Four... perhaps five days."

Rolan closed his eyes for a moment.

Five days.

The Beta army was already outside their walls.

Auregrad had not even covered half the distance.

"The Dwarven Holds?"

"Not intervening."

Rolan exhaled.

He had expected as much.

"The Dragon Throne?"

"Watching."

This answer came from the corner of the room.

An Elf stood beside the window.

Tall and slender, with long silver-white hair falling past his shoulders and a pair of thin spectacles resting on his face.

Eldren.

A Spellblade from Sylvaris who had arrived with a small Elven contingent.

"Dragonkind has no reason to involve itself," he continued. "This is a war between Humans and Demonkind."

Rolan looked at him.

"And the Elves?"

Eldren raised an eyebrow.

"We have an unfortunate habit of helping our neighbors before their houses burn to the ground."

"How many came with you?"

"One hundred and thirty-seven."

"Not many."

"You asked for help."

Eldren folded his arms.

"Not for all of Sylvaris to relocate to Asteron."

The door opened.

"Then add another forty-eight."

Rolan turned.

A man in silver-white armor stood in the doorway. His brown hair was disheveled from the journey. A scar crossed the left side of his face, passing over the eye without taking his sight.

Garling.

Forty-seven Adventurers stood behind him.

Yet everyone's attention went instead to the faint light visible through a gap in the armor near his shoulder.

A luminous pattern marked his skin.

**Rune Sigil.**

At last, Rolan smiled.

Barely.

"Finally."

Garling stepped inside.

"Good to see you too."

"How long can you fight?"

Garling looked at the map.

Then south.

The Beta drums had begun to sound.

"How long do you need us?"

Rolan answered without hesitation.

"As long as possible."

Garling smiled.

"Then try not to get us killed too quickly."

Eldren sighed.

"I've been in the same room as you two for only a few minutes, and I already regret coming here."

The drums stopped.

Rolan looked outside.

"Ready yourselves."

The first assault began.

---

"ARCHERS!"

Rolan stood atop the wall.

"READY!"

Hundreds of bows rose.

Ogres and Orcs charged across the plain.

"HOLD!"

One hundred meters.

Eighty.

Sixty.

Rolan dropped his hand.

"NOW!"

The sky darkened.

A rain of arrows struck the first ranks.

Dozens fell.

The next line stepped over them.

"SECOND VOLLEY!"

Arrows flew again.

The Beta army kept coming.

Siege ladders slammed against the walls.

Great stones sailed through the air.

One struck the western tower.

Stone and dust exploded outward.

"Third Company, west!"

"Commander, they're guarding the gate!"

"Fourth Company replaces them!"

"Fourth Company is at half strength!"

"Merge them with the militia!"

"The militia have never fought!"

Rolan stared at the officer.

"If this wall falls, they'll fight anyway."

He pointed west.

"So let them learn before the enemy reaches their homes."

Above the fort, Rolan's banner snapped in the wind.

Navy blue.

Ivory white.

Crimson.

As long as that banner remained visible, Asteron's defenders knew one thing.

The line had not broken.

---

On the eastern wall, an Ogre reached the battlements.

Its axe struck a soldier and hurled him aside.

The Ogre raised its weapon again.

Blue light flashed.

Its head fell.

Its massive body followed a moment later.

Eldren stood behind it.

A faint glow of Aethra ran along his blade.

An Elven archer shouted,

"Left!"

"I have eyes."

Eldren raised one hand.

A blue magic circle formed in the air.

A second appeared above a rank of Orcs.

He moved two fingers.

The air erupted.

Three Orcs were thrown from the wall.

An Eldorian soldier stared at him.

"You're a Mage?"

"No."

Eldren swung his sword and cut down the next enemy.

"Spellblade."

The assault lasted until sunset.

When the Beta forces finally withdrew, Asteron's defenders cheered.

Rolan did not.

He stood on the wall and watched thousands of campfires appear across the southern plain.

The Beta army was not leaving.

They were making camp.

Eldren came to stand beside him.

"They'll attack again tomorrow."

"I know."

"And the day after."

"I know."

Eldren studied his face.

"When does Auregrad arrive?"

"Four days."

Eldren looked back at the sea of fire below.

"Then we only need to make sure the fort still exists four days from now."

Rolan gave a thin smile.

"Easy, isn't it?"

"No."

For the first time that day, Eldren sounded entirely serious.

"Not at all."

---

## Day Two

They attacked before sunrise.

This time there was no trumpet.

No warning.

The sound of stone striking the walls woke men who had fallen asleep in their armor.

Garling fought on the western side from dawn until noon.

Then at the southern gate.

Then in the east.

Whenever the line came close to collapsing, his Rune Sigil appeared through the smoke.

The soldiers began to recognize its light.

If the Rune Sigil still shone, the Hero was still alive.

If the Hero was still alive, Asteron had not fallen.

But Garling was not the only reason the fort endured.

Whenever a unit lost its commander, someone arrived with new orders.

Rolan.

He barely raised a weapon on the second day.

He did not need to.

The army moved because of his voice.

Militia were paired with veterans.

Elven archers shifted with the direction of each attack.

Adventurers became a mobile force, sent wherever the defenses began to break.

Healers were divided among several positions so a single strike could not cripple all of Asteron's healing capability.

Near nightfall, Eldren found Rolan still standing over the map.

"When did you last sleep?"

"Don't remember."

"That isn't an answer."

"Then don't ask."

Eldren set a cup on the table.

Rolan looked at it.

"What's that?"

"Water."

"I was hoping for wine."

"If we survive, I'll buy you a barrel."

Rolan took the cup.

"Promise?"

"No."

Outside, the Beta drums sounded again.

Rolan set the cup down.

The second night was not over yet.

---

## Day Three

The first reinforcements appeared from the north at sunrise.

Solmaria's banners.

Verenza followed several hours later.

Asteron's defenders cheered as the northern gate opened.

Hundreds of fresh soldiers entered the fort.

Knights.

Healers.

Infantry.

Wagons of medicine.

Food.

Arrows.

For the first time since the siege began, Rolan thought they might truly hold.

The feeling lasted only a few hours.

"Solmaria's troops refuse to leave the northern sector."

"What?"

"They were ordered to protect the evacuation route."

"The evacuation is nearly complete."

"Those are their kingdom's orders."

Rolan rubbed his face.

"Verenza?"

"They want their own sector command."

"Why?"

"They say their troops aren't under an Eldorian officer."

Rolan stared at the map.

Hundreds of new soldiers had arrived.

And somehow the defense had become more complicated.

Different kingdoms.

Different chains of command.

Different ways of fighting.

And no one had the authority to command them all.

Garling stood in the doorway.

"You can command them."

Rolan gave a short laugh.

"I'm not even a noble."

"They don't need a noble."

Garling looked at the map.

"They need someone who knows what's happening."

"I don't have the authority."

From the corner, Eldren closed his book.

"If the fort falls, you can explain the finer points of authority to their corpses."

Rolan looked at both of them.

"Why is it that whenever you two agree, my life gets harder?"

Garling smiled.

"Because it usually does."

That day, Rolan began giving orders to forces that were not officially under his command.

Strangely—

they began to listen.

---

## Day Four

Rain fell.

Blood ran with the water between Asteron's stones.

The Beta army did not stop.

They changed targets.

West.

East.

The gate.

Then west again.

Eldren watched from one of the remaining towers.

An assault from the west.

A feint in the east.

Rotating troops.

Fresh units committed precisely when the previous ones began to tire.

They were testing the defenses.

Searching for patterns.

Draining strength.

This was no wild army.

No horde of monsters that happened to move together.

There was strategy behind every attack.

A single will directed it all.

Eldren looked toward the Beta camp.

Far away, a deep crimson banner stood among thousands of tents.

**β**

Yet for four days—

the person behind all of this had never appeared on the battlefield.

Eldren narrowed his eyes.

"So you still haven't shown yourself..."

For the first time, he understood something more troubling than the size of the Beta army.

The one directing this entire war—

**had not yet needed to take the field.**

---

On the fourth night, Rolan walked through the fortress courtyard.

There were no children anymore.

Most civilians had been evacuated.

Those who remained were people who refused to leave wounded family members, workers assisting the Healers, smiths repairing weapons, and soldiers.

Rolan stopped beside a wall.

Garling sat against it.

His eyes were closed.

"Garling."

No answer.

Rolan stepped closer.

"Garling?"

The Hero opened one eye.

"I'm alive."

"Good."

"I was sleeping."

"In the courtyard?"

"I didn't have time to find a bed."

Rolan sat beside him.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Garling asked,

"How many?"

Rolan knew what he meant.

"More than a thousand."

Garling opened his eyes.

"And them?"

"More."

"That doesn't make me feel better."

"It wasn't meant to."

They fell silent again.

A smith's hammer rang somewhere in the distance.

Even at midnight, the forges were still working.

Garling looked at the sky.

"How much longer?"

"Auregrad may arrive tomorrow."

"Good."

Rolan looked at the cracked wall.

"Let's hope there's still an Asteron when they do."

---

## Day Five

Auregrad's banners appeared near midday.

The cavalry came first.

Behind them marched infantry and Knights.

No army descended from the sky.

There was no Wyvern Corps.

In those days, Auregrad had not yet become the military power later generations would know.

They came to war as other kingdoms did.

With horses.

Infantry.

Knights.

And soldiers who had marched for days to reach Asteron.

Auregrad's cavalry immediately struck the eastern side of the Beta army.

For several hours, the battlefield changed.

The Beta forces gave ground.

Asteron's soldiers cheered.

Garling even laughed when he saw Auregrad's banners.

"Finally."

Rolan did not laugh.

He watched the Beta lines.

They were retreating too cleanly.

"Tell the cavalry to stop."

An officer stared at him.

"They're winning."

"Tell them to stop!"

Too late.

The Beta line opened.

Ogres carrying heavy spears emerged from behind it.

Auregrad's cavalry lost its momentum.

Then Orcs moved in from both flanks.

A trap.

Cheers became screams.

Rolan clenched his fist.

Whoever commanded the Beta army had anticipated their arrival.

Perhaps even waited for it.

Auregrad had finally come.

And the enemy had turned their arrival into a weapon.

By nightfall, Auregrad's forces returned to Asteron with their strength badly reduced.

The fifth day ended without victory.

---

## Day Six

There was no part of Asteron left undamaged.

The western wall was cracked.

The eastern tower had fallen.

The southern gate had been reinforced with timber, stone, wagons—anything they could find.

Healers worked until their Aethra was nearly exhausted.

Broken swords were repaired.

Armor beyond repair was replaced with equipment taken from those who no longer needed it.

Soldiers slept sitting upright.

Some did not wake when the trumpet sounded.

Garling had stopped counting the wounds on his body.

Rolan had lost his voice and now sent some orders through messengers.

Eldren stood atop the remains of the western tower when he felt it.

He closed his eyes.

Aethra.

For six days, thousands had used magic.

Healers healed.

Mages attacked.

Spellblades reinforced their weapons.

Garling's Rune Sigil had remained active.

And from the direction of the Beta army—

something far greater had been pressing against the flow of Aethra around the battlefield.

Eldren had not seen its source.

But he could feel it.

So much Aethra had been released in one place.

Too much.

Eldren opened his eyes.

Something had changed.

Very slightly.

Almost imperceptibly.

But it had changed.

The Aethra around Asteron no longer flowed as it should.

"Eldren!"

He turned.

An archer called from below.

"The enemy is withdrawing!"

Eldren looked south.

It was true.

The Beta forces were pulling back.

For the first time in six days, the plain before Asteron was empty.

Some soldiers began to cheer.

Eldren did not.

Thousands of Beta troops formed new ranks far to the south.

Not attacking.

Not retreating.

Waiting.

Then their center opened.

The Ogres on either side lowered their weapons.

The Orcs followed.

One by one.

Then hundreds.

Then thousands.

The entire army bowed its head.

Eldren adjusted his spectacles.

The cynical smile that usually touched his face vanished.

"Ah."

He stared at the empty corridor opening through the heart of the Beta army.

"So you've finally come yourself."

---

## Day Seven

Morning came without an attack.

No arrows.

No stones striking the walls.

Even the Beta drums were silent.

Thousands of warriors simply stood on the southern plain.

Waiting.

Asteron's defenders watched from what remained of the walls.

No one understood what they were waiting for.

Then—

the Beta ranks parted.

Someone walked out.

His steps were slow.

Unhurried.

Yet every warrior he passed lowered their head.

Ogres.

Orcs.

Tribal chiefs.

Officers.

All of them.

No one on Asteron's walls spoke.

The figure drew closer.

Only then could they see him clearly.

He towered even among the Ogres.

Deep crimson skin.

Long silver-white hair.

Two black horns rising from his head.

Layered dark armor covered his massive, muscular frame.

A colossal kanabō rested in his hand.

He stopped.

Then raised his face toward Asteron.

Red light slowly appeared across his body.

Garling's Rune Sigil reacted.

The Hero looked down at the light on his own skin.

"What...?"

Eldren froze.

Rolan gripped his banner.

The red light on the figure's body formed a single symbol.

**β**

A soldier on the wall stepped back.

"Demon Lord..."

Silence spread.

Eldren stared at the figure below.

For once, even he had no cynical remark.

Rolan spoke only one name.

"Raizen."

Several soldiers turned toward him.

The name spread in whispers along the wall.

Raizen.

**The Oni King.**

**Demon Lord Beta.**

For six days, he had commanded the war without entering the battlefield.

His army had nearly destroyed Asteron without him ever needing to raise a weapon.

On the seventh day—

he finally advanced.

Raizen raised his kanabō.

For the first time that morning, the Beta drums sounded.

**DOOM.**

Thousands of weapons rose.

**DOOM.**

The Ogres moved.

**DOOM.**

The Orcs began to run.

Raizen lowered the kanabō.

The entire plain surged forward.

The final assault had begun.

---

The western outer wall collapsed before noon.

Beta forces poured through the breach.

Garling went down to meet them.

His Rune Sigil blazed.

He cut down an Ogre.

Then an Orc.

Then another.

But this was not the Garling of the first day.

His breathing was heavy.

His armor was cracked.

Every movement tore old wounds open again.

Yet when the soldiers saw his Rune Sigil—

they followed.

"HERO!"

"HOLD THE LINE!"

"DO NOT FALL BACK!"

Then the Beta soldiers stopped.

One by one, they opened a path.

Raizen walked through them.

For the first time—

Garling stood face-to-face with the man who had made Asteron bleed for seven days.

Raizen looked at the fallen Demonkind around him.

Then at Garling's Rune Sigil.

"So."

His voice was deep.

Calm.

"This is their Hero."

Garling raised his sword.

His hand trembled with exhaustion.

"And you must be the man who's kept me from sleeping for a week."

Raizen gave a faint smile.

"I expected more."

He moved.

Garling barely raised his sword in time.

The kanabō struck the blade.

**DOOM!**

The ground split.

Garling was hurled into the wall.

Asteron's cheers died.

Raizen lowered the kanabō.

"Get up."

Garling coughed.

Blood ran from his lips.

Then he laughed.

"Yeah."

He forced himself upright.

"This is going to be troublesome."

---

Rolan watched from a distance.

One strike.

Only one.

And the difference was already clear.

Garling would not win alone.

"Second Company!"

Several soldiers turned.

"Form up!"

"Commander, what about the wall?"

Rolan took up his banner.

"We aren't abandoning the defensive line."

He looked at Raizen.

"We're moving it."

---

Eldren saw Rolan descend.

Then Garling.

"Stupid Human."

He drew his sword.

"Two stupid Humans."

Then followed them down.

---

Raizen raised his kanabō.

Rolan's spear struck the side of his armor.

Raizen stopped.

He turned.

"And you?"

Rolan pulled the spear back.

"The man making sure he doesn't die."

Garling raised one hand from the ground.

"Thank you."

"I haven't saved you yet."

"It's the thought that counts."

Raizen laughed.

"Good."

A blue magic circle appeared beneath his feet.

Eldren landed several steps behind them.

"If you're finished introducing yourselves..."

A second circle formed.

Then a third.

"I would like this war to end today."

Light erupted from the ground.

Raizen was driven back one step.

Only one.

But Eldren saw it.

The flow of Aethra.

The Beta Sigil.

When Raizen attacked, Aethra flowed into his weapon and arm.

When he defended, the flow reversed to reinforce his body.

Attack.

Defense.

Attack.

Defense.

Between them—

a gap.

Almost nothing.

"Rolan!"

"What?"

"Make him attack with everything he has."

"And then?"

"I'll disrupt his Aethra flow."

"For how long?"

Eldren studied Raizen.

"Less than a second."

Rolan stared at him.

"That's your plan?"

"I'm a Scholar."

Eldren adjusted his spectacles.

"Not a miracle worker."

Garling wiped blood from his mouth.

"One second is enough."

"You're sure?"

Garling smiled.

"No."

Rolan raised his spear.

"Good."

He looked at Raizen.

"I hate plans that are too easy."

---

Rolan's banner moved forward.

The soldiers saw it.

Eldoria.

Solmaria.

Verenza.

Astrelia.

Auregrad.

Adventurers.

Elves.

For days they had fought as forces from different lands.

That day—

they followed the same banner.

Not because a king ordered them to.

Not because a treaty united them.

That treaty did not even exist yet.

They did it because if the line broke now, every sacrifice before it would mean nothing.

Rolan charged.

Raizen raised his kanabō.

"NOW!"

Eldren drove his sword into the ground.

Three magic circles opened.

Every trace of Aethra he could still command poured into them.

Raizen swung the kanabō.

Rolan blocked.

His spear shattered.

His body flew backward.

But the attack had forced Raizen to commit his strength.

Eldren's magic circles flared.

The Aethra around the Oni King lurched.

The Beta Sigil reacted.

The Aethra committed to attack reversed toward defense.

For a fraction of a second—

neither was complete.

"GARLING!"

The Rune Sigil blazed.

Garling moved.

Raizen saw him.

Too late.

The sword pierced armor.

Pierced flesh.

Raizen struck Garling away with one hand.

The Hero was thrown aside.

But his sword remained buried in Raizen's chest.

The Oni King stepped back.

Once.

Twice.

He gripped the blade running through him.

The battlefield fell silent.

Raizen was still standing.

The Beta army began to move.

The Oni King raised one hand.

They stopped.

He looked at Garling.

Then Rolan.

Then Eldren.

"So..."

Blood ran from the corner of his mouth.

"This is the strength your people chose."

Garling drew a breath.

"No."

Raizen looked at him.

Garling glanced at his two companions.

"This is the strength we chose together."

Raizen fell silent.

Then he laughed softly.

"I see."

His knee touched the ground.

The light of **β** flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then Raizen fell.

**Demon Lord Beta was dead.**

---

For several seconds—

there was no sound.

Then cheers erupted across Asteron.

They had won.

After seven days.

After thousands of casualties.

After shattered walls and armies from different kingdoms brought to the brink.

They had won.

But Eldren did not cheer.

He stared at the ground.

The **β** on Raizen's body grew brighter.

"What...?"

The Beta Sigil broke apart into motes of light.

Then vanished.

And the Aethra around Asteron—

changed.

Eldren froze.

For days, he had sensed the signs.

Now the entire flow changed at once.

The Aethra of thousands of living beings.

The remnants of magic released throughout the battle.

Rune Sigil.

Demon Sigil.

Blood.

Death.

Everything converged.

But it did not return to its former pattern.

The Aethra began to deviate.

"Garling!"

The Hero turned.

"Get away from there!"

Cracks of light spread through the ground.

Trees in the distance shook though there was no wind.

From the Monster Forest came a roar.

One.

Dozens.

Then hundreds.

Magic lamps along the walls flickered erratically.

Water surged from the wells and turned murky.

Rolan struggled to his feet.

"What's happening?"

"Aethra."

"What's wrong with it?"

For the first time since they had met, Rolan saw fear on Eldren's face.

"The flow is wrong."

A tower collapsed.

Screams rose from inside Asteron.

Rolan looked at the fortress they had spent seven days defending.

Then he picked up his broken banner.

"EVACUATE ASTERON!"

An officer stared at him in disbelief.

"Commander... we won!"

Rolan seized him by the collar.

"Then make sure someone stays alive to remember it."

He released him.

"EVERYONE TO THE NORTHERN GATE!"

---

That night, the Beta army withdrew south.

There was no pursuit.

Their leader was dead.

The army that had come to open the road into Eldoria returned without its Demon Lord.

To the north, lines of Humans and Elves abandoned Asteron.

Some wept.

Some no longer had the strength to weep.

Soldiers carried wounded comrades.

Healers walked while supporting one another.

Knights from different kingdoms helped civilians onto wagons.

The banners of many kingdoms moved along the same road.

On a small hill, Garling stood with the aid of his sword.

Rolan sat beside what remained of his banner.

Eldren watched strange light move among Asteron's ruins.

"Will it stop?" Garling asked.

Eldren did not answer.

"Eldren?"

"I don't know."

Rolan turned.

It might have been the first time he had ever heard the Elf say those words.

Eldren removed his spectacles.

Cleaned them.

Put them back on.

"I don't even know what I'm looking at."

Rolan laughed softly, then winced at his wounds.

"Good."

Eldren looked at him.

"What part of that is good?"

"If even you don't know, at least I don't have to feel stupid."

Garling laughed.

Eldren sighed.

"You two are truly insufferable."

They fell silent.

Below them stood the fortress they had managed to defend.

And the home they had failed to save.

At last, Garling asked,

"Was all of this worth it?"

Rolan looked toward the northern road.

Thousands were walking away from Asteron.

Alive.

"Look at them."

Garling followed his gaze.

"Then ask me again tomorrow."

## After Asteron

History would remember those seven days as the **Battle of Asteron**.

Demon Lord Beta was slain.

His army was driven back south.

The invasion ended.

But Asteron left behind a question far greater than who had won the battle.

The Human kingdoms had come to Eldoria's aid.

But they had arrived at different times.

They had fought together.

But they had no unified command.

Royal orders clashed with the needs of the battlefield.

Forces that should have supported one another sometimes lacked even the authority to accept orders from the same commander.

After the war, the five crowns finally sat at the same table.

Astrelia.

Verenza.

Solmaria.

Eldoria.

Auregrad.

One question hung over their meeting.

**Why had it taken days before five kingdoms could stand on the same battlefield?**

They did not want to face that question again when the next great invasion came.

From their agreement, an alliance was born.

**The Grand Concord.**

Auregrad did not forget Asteron either.

Its forces had come with horses, infantry, and Knights.

They had arrived late.

And even after they arrived, they had been overwhelmed.

In the decades that followed, the kingdom began to change the way it understood war.

Its forces grew more organized.

Faster.

Stronger.

Later generations would come to know Auregrad as the Military Kingdom.

But the changes brought by Asteron did not end with Human politics.

Asteron itself never truly recovered.

Scholars later gave a name to the phenomenon Eldren had first perceived.

**Aethra Distortion.**

The Aethra in the region had not vanished.

It had not been corrupted.

It had not become something evil.

Its flow had deviated.

Magic became unstable.

Crops struggled to grow.

Water changed without any clear cause.

Monsters from the part of the Monster Forest bordering Asteron became increasingly aggressive and began roaming farther from their habitats.

The region around the fort gained a new name.

**The Aethra Scar.**

Eldoria tried several times to reclaim Asteron.

Each attempt failed.

Soldiers could still enter the region.

Adventurers could still explore its ruins.

But no settlement could survive there for long.

With Beta deprived of its Demon Lord and fractured in the south, Asteron's value as a defensive line diminished.

Eldoria eventually pulled its defenses north.

The fortress that had stopped a Demon Lord was abandoned.

Years passed.

Stone roads disappeared beneath the grass.

Homes collapsed.

Tree roots pierced the walls.

The names of the fallen slowly eroded beneath the rain.

New generations grew up knowing Asteron only from history books.

The fortress became a ruin.

The ruin became a forbidden place.

And the forbidden place—

was slowly forgotten.

Yet the Aethra in Asteron never returned to what it had been.

Scholars came to study it.

Mages tried to measure its flow.

Expeditions searched the ruins for the cause of the disturbance.

They found many things.

Remnants of weapons.

Collapsed passages.

Nameless graves.

Even relics believed to have been there long before Fort Asteron was built.

But no one found an answer.

Why had the Aethra Distortion never disappeared?

Why did its flow continue to change?

And why, in some parts of the ruins, did Aethra seem to move according to patterns unknown to modern magic?

Eventually, the expeditions stopped coming.

New maps began omitting the road to Asteron.

The forest reclaimed the fields.

Monsters wandered among empty buildings.

Forty-three years passed.

No guards remained at the gate.

No banners flew above the walls.

No children's voices filled the streets.

Only old stones, trees, and silence remained.

And yet, among the ruins—

something still moved.

Slowly.

Almost imperceptibly.

Like a heartbeat stirring once after years of silence.

Then fading again.

As though nothing had happened at all.

And Asteron—

**still waited.**
